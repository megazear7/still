# A quieter place to keep a draft

Most writing software wants to become the place your words live. It has a library, a sync story, a share sheet, and a small celebration every time a file hits the disk. That machinery is useful until the work itself is long, unfinished, and already sitting in a folder of Markdown you can open with anything.

Still is a Mac app for that folder. It shows one note at a time, as prose rather than as source, and it writes the file while you type. There is no save indicator. The absence is the point. A quiet tool should not keep announcing that it is working.

## The desk, not the platform

You point Still at one or more folders. Those folders stay yours. The sidebar can show the tree, and you can drag a note into another folder, rename it, duplicate it, or throw it away after a second click so a slip of the pointer does not erase a chapter. Close the app and the same folders, the same expanded branches, and the same open note are there when you come back.

The other side of that sidebar is the shape of the piece you are in. Headings remain headings. Paragraphs sit underneath them, one line each, as much of the sentence as the column can hold. A run of short lines, the kind that show up in a scene heading or a scrap of dialogue, is gathered into a single entry so the map does not turn into a stack of fragments. A long paragraph stays itself. Hover if you need a little more of the sentence. Click, and the page moves to that passage and flashes it, just enough to answer the question you actually had: which paragraph was that?

## Finding a line without leaving the page

Search lives in the corner and stays faint until you use it. Command-F puts the cursor there. You can look across every file in the folders you opened, or stay inside the note on screen.

Inside one note, the matches are marked in the prose. The menu tells you where you are, “3 of 16,” and lists that match together with the ones that follow, up to ten. Enter walks forward. Shift-Enter walks back. Choosing a row jumps there, and the list starts again from the line you chose. When you are done, a small × clears the field. Nothing else on the page changes color to celebrate the fact that you searched.

## What we kept out

A lot of the work was deciding what would make the room noisier. One file, not a row of tabs. Rendered sentences, not a split view of hashes and brackets, with line spacing you can sit in for an hour. A dark cream page instead of a bright white one. Autosave with no toast, because a toast is a tap on the shoulder.

The files are ordinary Markdown. Images that live beside them can sit in the note. Tables, lists, and links are there because a draft sometimes needs them, not because the app is trying to become a publisher.

Still is built for a person who already knows where the work is. The folder is the archive. The app is just a calmer way to be inside one file.
